# Chaos Temple (FF1) — Analysis

**Status: opening key determined (2026-08-01, key sweep).**

## Opening key: E minor

Derived without opening the chord track (hidden as "cheating" — same
derive-then-verify discipline as Cave). Evidence path:

1. Bass opens on a long, sustained E.
2. The arpeggio track spells an E minor triad as the opening harmony — the
   progression read as Em → D → C → D, with the return to Em correctly
   predicted before re-checking the screen.
3. The melody dwells on E.

Points 2 and 3 are independent of the first-bass-note rule, so this song is
a legitimate test case for the sweep hypothesis (a hit — see key-sweep.md).

Method note: the initial two-step argument — E is the tonic by position and
emphasis, G supplies the third and says minor — is sound *as a two-step*:
position/emphasis first, then the third read against that tonic. (Emphasis
on G alone would be equally consistent with G major; the order of the steps
is what makes it work.) It was stated as inconclusive pending the other
tracks, which is the right discipline.

## Device log — compound lines, third sighting

A single monophonic track carrying two strands at once, one moving and one
repeating: seen in the Cave bass dance, the Cave melody (sustained low note
under a moving upper line), and now the Chaos Temple arpeggio. Given the
NES's three-pitched-voice ceiling, this looks like a core technique for
faking more voices than the hardware allows — directly on target for the
README's stated goal.
