# Battle (FF1) — Analysis

**Status: opening key determined (2026-08-01, key sweep). Only the bass has
been read; melody and other tracks unexamined.**

## Opening key: G minor

Josh's derivation, from the bass alone:

1. Bass sits on a repeating low pattern built on G, with a recurring drop to
   F — G hammered many times, F only visited. The *emphasis* (how often, how
   long, on which beats) is itself evidence for tonic, on top of which
   pitches are present.
2. The bass ostinato states a minor 3rd above the tonic, so mode is settled
   without a vertical check — the reason a bass-only derivation is
   defensible here in a way it wasn't on Airship.
3. Later, large stepwise descending runs, diatonic to G minor **except for a
   raised 7th**, identified immediately.

Method note (logged deliberately): Josh predicted minor before reading, on
genre grounds — "it's a battle theme, darker, scarier." Correct here, but a
prior, not evidence.

## Open Questions

**1. Is the raised 7th a scale, or a consequence?**

Current framing: the song "occasionally goes to G harmonic minor." The notes
behind that are right; the verb may not be. The alternative framing (general
theory, to be tested against this song): the raised 7th in a minor key is
usually not a scale the composer switches to but a *byproduct of a chord* —
a minor key wants a functioning dominant, a dominant needs a major third,
and that third is the raised 7th. The scale falls out of the chord's
requirements rather than the other way round.

Falsifiable prediction: the raised 7th should cluster around one particular
chord and be largely absent elsewhere. Test: locate every raised 7th, note
what the bass is doing at that moment, see whether the distribution is
clustered or scattered. If scattered, "the melodic material genuinely draws
on harmonic minor as a scale" becomes the better description — a real
finding, not a fallback.

**2. The note Josh hasn't caught yet.**

At least one of the large descending bass runs contains a pitch that belongs
to neither G natural minor nor G harmonic minor. Josh wants to find it
himself — it is deliberately not named here. It bears directly on question 1.
(Relevant concept already known: the ascending/descending asymmetry in
minor-scale practice.)

**3. Scope caveat.**

Only the bass has been read. Both questions above may look different once
the melody and other tracks are open.

**4. "First bass note" definition (sweep-wide, surfaced by this song).**

The bass track does not begin in bar 1 — another track carries an opening

scores as a hit under the first definition only; the convention needs
